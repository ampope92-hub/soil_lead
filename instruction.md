The lab sent back the Bristol east allotments survey and we need the over-limit list rebuilt before it goes to the council. Their export is in `/app/data`: the instrument results in run order, the certified reference materials that ran with them, the sample register, the policy, and `/app/data/split_checks.csv`. That last one is a dozen samples we split, with a second portion sent to the reference lab. Eight carry the lab's figure beside our reading, and four are still with the lab. The README describes each file and quotes the lab's own count of results above the limit. That's for you, not the program, it is not one of the files your script is given.

We work in TypeScript on node. The program is one file, `/app/screen/src/screen.ts`, and there is a stub there to start from. We compile it ourselves with our own settings, so nothing else in the project is used. What it has to do:

- `node /app/screen/dist/screen.js <data-dir> <output-json>` writes one JSON object with exactly three keys. `over_limit` is the ids out of `results.csv` you are reporting above `limit_mg_kg` in `policy.json`, with no id appearing twice. `split_check` holds your figure for each of the samples named in that survey's `split_checks.csv` and nothing else, one number each under the sample's id, the four the lab has not reported on included. `corrected_mg_kg` holds your figure for every sample in `results.csv`, one number under each sample's id, and it is what over_limit is decided from.
- imports are limited to node's own modules, there are no packages on the box and no network
- the same program then runs against a second survey at another site, the same five files and a README in the same format, with its own reference materials, limit and policy, so it has to work from whichever directory it is handed
- the five files in `/app/data` stay as shipped, and we check them against our copy

The reference lab's split results are what this survey is held to: the correction we accept is the one that puts the split samples where the lab has them, and that same corrected figure is what decides every sample on the list. We compare your `split_check` figures against what the lab returned for the same soil, the four pending ones included, and each has to land within `split_check_tolerance` of the lab's figure, read as a fraction of it. The two have to agree: a split sample whose figure you report above the limit belongs in `over_limit`, and one you report at or below it does not, and the same holds for corrected_mg_kg against every sample on the list, split or not. The lab ran the soil as it arrived, wet, and its results column is on that basis, while the limit and everything the reference lab returns are dry weight, and the register in samples.csv has the moisture of every sample. No sample in either survey sits exactly on the limit, and no sample has more than one reading the lab accepted. Every sample also went back to the lab for confirmation and the list itself is scored against what came back, and so is corrected_mg_kg itself for every sample split_checks.csv does not already cover, each held to `corrected_tolerance` of the lab's figure the way a split figure is held to `split_check_tolerance`: of the samples you list, the share confirmed over the limit has to reach `minimum_precision`, and of the samples the lab put over the limit your list has to hold at least `minimum_recall`. We read those four figures from the policy of whichever survey is being scored.
